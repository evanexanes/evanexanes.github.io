03 — Before you write

Questions that come up first

Five things people ask before they send the first message. If yours is not here, the form above is the faster route.

What kind of sites do you build?

The kinds in the work above: multi-page company sites, editorial sites with a growing archive, small catalogue stores and one-page personal sites. They are different problems — a company site is judged on how quickly a stranger finds the right service page, a blog on whether a long post stays readable on a phone, a store on whether the product page answers the question the buyer actually had.

WordPress or hand-written code — how do you decide?

By who has to touch the page next. If your team publishes weekly, WordPress and Elementor with named custom fields wins, because filling in a field is a task anyone can learn. If a page has to be exact and will rarely change, hand-written HTML and CSS is smaller, faster and has less to go wrong.

It is a decision about the next two years of the site, not about which tool I prefer.

Can my team update the site after you hand it over?

That is the point of how I build. Anything that repeats runs on a template, anything editable is a named field rather than text buried in a layout, and the handover is written down. A new service page should be filled in, not rebuilt.

Do you work remotely?

Yes. I am based in Metro Manila and work remotely, including with teams several time zones away. Written updates carry the work — what changed, what is next, what I need from you — so progress does not depend on us being awake at the same time.

Do you do the SEO too, or only the build?

Both, and I would rather not separate them. A site that loads fast and reads clearly has already done most of its own SEO; the rest is technical work that never shows on the page — structured data so a crawler is told what a page is instead of inferring it, slugs audited before a rename breaks every inbound link, and redirects that resolve in one hop rather than a chain.

That work now has a second audience. An AI assistant answering a question has to parse your page to quote it, and it quotes what is marked up far more reliably than what is merely laid out. Optimising for that is what AEO and GEO mean. The FAQ you are reading carries its own FAQPage JSON-LD for exactly this reason — view the source of this page and it is there.

What do you need from me to start?

What the site is for, who it is for, and what has to be on it. From that we agree the pages and how they are organised before anything is designed — structure first is the cheapest place to change your mind.
